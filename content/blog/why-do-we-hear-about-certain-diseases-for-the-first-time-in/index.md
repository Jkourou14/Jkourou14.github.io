Have you ever noticed that some diseases seem to be much more commonly discussed in countries like the USA, while you may have barely heard of them elsewhere?

Does that mean people in other countries don’t get these diseases? 

Not necessarily.. Many diseases that are well recognized and diagnosed in the USA can be found anywhere in the world.

So, if that’s the case… What explains the difference?

The answer is actually quite simple “the power of diagnosis”.

Countries with strong healthcare systems often have greater access to diagnostic tools, laboratory tests, imaging technologies, genetic testing, specialized clinicians, and screening programs. They also have more ressources to invest in developing and implementing these technologies.

And the equation becomes surprisingly simple “The more you diagnose, the more you discover”.

A disease can exist within a population for years without being properly identified. Patients may be given a different diagnosis, their symptoms may be attributed to something else, or they may never have access to the appropriate testing in the first place.

This is why i believe that one of the most important steps in medicine is often understimated “Getting diagnosis right”.

The first efficient step toward effective treatment should be a sharp, accurate, and timely diagnosis. Because the more precise the diagnosis, the more precise the treatment can become.

And sometimes, what looks like a difference in disease prevalence between countries may partly be a difference in what we have the ability to detect. 

Diagnosis doesn’t just name the disease, it makes the invisible visible.

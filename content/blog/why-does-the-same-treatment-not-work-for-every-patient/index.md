We often hear terms like “personalized medicine”, “targeted therapy”, and “precision medicine” as if they simply mean “choosing a treatment for a patient”.

It sounds simple… But the reality behind these concepts is far more complex than it seems.

So what exactly is personalized medicine? Why is the future of medicine increasingly depending on it? And how can two patients with the same diagnosis respond completely differently to the same treatment? These are all the questions modern medicine keeps trying to answer.

For decades, medicine has often followed a “one-size-fits-all” approach, which basically treats patients with the same disease with the same treatment following the same protocol… Well, that’s wrong because biology doesn’t work that way.

Two patients may both have the same cancer type, the same stage, and receive the same drug. Yet, one patient responds remarkably well, while the other develops severe side effects or no response at all. 

WHY? 

Because every human body is biologically unique. We differ by our genetics, our mutations, our metabolism, our immune systems that behave differently, and even the molecular pathways driving diseases can vary from one patient to another.

Basically, biology is like identity; each of us has a unique identity.

Here comes the crucial role of “Precision Medicine”,” which seeks to understand these differences rather than ignore them.

 Rather than treating diseases as identical conditions in every patient, It aims to study the individual biological profile behind the disease itself. This includes genetic mutations, biomarkers, immune responses, and even how a person metabolizes a drug.

The purpose is not only to treat a disease, but also to identify the treatment most likely to work for a specific patient.

This is crucial because personalized medicine could offer fewer side effects, more effective therapies, avoiding unnecessary treatments, earlier interventions, and therapies designed to target the exact biological problem involved. 

 In oncology, especially, precision medicine is already transforming how treatments are selected. Some therapies now specifically target particular mutations or molecular pathways rather than attacking all rapidly dividing cells indiscriminately. 

And this is one of the biggest shifts happening in modern healthcare. The future of medicine is no longer only about treating diseases alone… But about understanding the biology of each individual person.

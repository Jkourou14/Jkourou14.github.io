With the rise of AI, computational biology, molecular modelling, and all the amazing tools we have today, we might get the impression that we no longer need lab experiments, simply because we can sit behind a computer, analyze some data, run a simulation, and call it a day. 

Yet, can we just replace the laboratory with a computer?

Well, the answer is MYTH, Bioinformatics doesn’t replace experiments. 

In fact, It helps us decide what to test, why to test it, and sometimes how to test it more efficiently.

Imagine you want to identify molecules that could interact with a specific protein. Instead of experimentally testing thousands of molecules one by one, bioinformatics can help you narrow down the candidates.

You can analyze sequences, predict structures, perform docking, run molecular dynamics simulations, and identify promising candidates.

But here comes the important part “A prediction is still a prediction.”

A molecule may look like a great candidate *in silico* and completely fail experimentally; simply because biology is complicated. 

Cells, proteins and biological systems don't always behave exactly as our models predict.

And that's why bioinformatics and experimental biology shouldn't be seen as competitors. They work together.

Bioinformatics generates hypotheses. Then, Experiments test them…Experimental results improve our understanding and our models…And the cycle continues…

The same goes in the opposite direction.

Experiments can generate enormous amounts of data, but without bioinformatics, making sense of millions of sequences, expression profiles or molecular measurements would be extremely difficult.

So no, bioinformatics isn't here to make the laboratory obsolete, It's here to make research smarter, more efficient and more informed.

The real power is the combination, the computer can suggest what might happen…but biology gets the final word.

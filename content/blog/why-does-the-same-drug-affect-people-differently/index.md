Imagine traveling abroad and taking a medication that seems much stronger, or much weaker, than what you are used to… 

Or consider two patients receiving the same treatment, at the same dose, for the same disease, yet experiencing completely different outcomes, one recovers quickly, the other experiences side effects, and perhaps, a third person feels almost no effect at all…

How is that possible if they all took the same medication?

It is a concept called “pharmacogenomics”.

Pharmacogenomics is the field that studies how genetic variations influence an individual’s response to medications. It helps us understand why a drug may be highly effective for one patient, ineffective for another, or even cause adverse effects in some individuals. 

In simple words, it asks ‘what if the same pill is not meant to work the same way for everyone?’

For a long time, medicine has been ‘one dose fits all’, yet our biology doesn’t work like that… Each of us carries small genetic differences that can change how we metabolize a drug, how fast we eliminate it, or even whether a treatment will work at all.

Some people break down medications too fast, so the drug becomes less effective. Others break them down too slowly, so the drug builds up and causes side effects. While some may not respond at all because the biological target is slightly different. 

Pharmacogenomics tries to read these differences before treatment begins, to make medicine more precise, more personal, and more human…

It sits at the heart of personalized medicine, with one clear goal “delivering the right drug to the right patient, at the right dose, at the right time.”

It is already being used in areas like cancer therapy, psychiatry, and cardiovascular diseases, where the difference between “standard dose” and “right dose” can completely change a patient’s journey.

This is just the start… In the future, a simple genetic test could guide doctors, before therapy even starts,  in choosing not just the drug, but the dose, and sometimes even avoiding treatments that would not work.

For me, pharmacogenomics is not just a scientific concept.. It feels like a shift in mindset, from treating diseases in general, to understanding people in detail.

Because every treatment begins not with a disease, but with a unique human biology.

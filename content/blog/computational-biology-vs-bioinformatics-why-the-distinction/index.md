As someone working at the intersection of biology and computation, I often notice how easily Bioinformatics and Computational Biology are used as if they mean the same thing. They are closely related. They collaborate constantly. But they are NOT IDENTICAL.

And understanding the difference changes the way we think about modern biomedical research.

Bioinformatics is, at its core, about biological data. It focuses on organizing, processing, and analyzing large-scale datasets (from sequences to expression profiles to structural information).

It asks a fundamental question: "What does the data reveal?"

In contrast, Computational Biology leans more toward modeling and interpretation. It involves building mathematical or computational representations of biological systems, simulating interactions, and exploring mechanisms.

It asks a different question: "How and Why does this system behave the way it does?"

In brief, one (Bioinformatics) extracts patterns, the other (Computational Biology) seeks explanation.

In reality, the most meaningful advances in areas like precision medicine, AI-driven biomedical research don't belong exclusively to one domain. In fact, they emerge where rigorous data analysis meets conceptual biological modeling.

Perhaps the real distinction is less about separation, and more about PERSPECTIVE.

I would be curious to hear how others in the field perceive this boundary. Is it clear, or is it gradually dissolving?

On this International Workers’ Day, I’ve been thinking about something quietly present in our generation. We don’t just work hard. We carry the constant need to “be more, do more, prove more”.

Somewhere along the way, work stopped being just “WHAT WE DO”…and slowly became “WHO WE ARE”. 

We tie our worth to productivity. We measure our days by how much we achieve. And when we rest, it doesn’t always feel like rest; it feels like guilt.

As if slowing down means falling behind, and pausing means we are not doing enough.

And for many women, this pressure often carries an additional weight. The need to prove ourselves. To be taken seriously. To be competent, strong, present, sometimes all at once. To succeed professionally, while still being expected to hold everything together in other parts of life.

We learnt how to work hard. How to push through. How to stay disciplined, consistent, ambitious…But we were rarely taught how to rest without questioning ourselves.

Behind the visible achievements, there is often something quieter pressure, exhaustion, self-doubt… A constant feeling that there is always more to do, more to become.

And the hardest part is that from the outside, everything can look perfectly fine.

In a world that rewards speed, productivity, and constant output, choosing to slow down can feel uncomfortable, almost unfamiliar.

But maybe the question is not whether we are working hard enough. Maybe the question is: “**at what cost are we doing all of this?”**

Because working hard is not the problem. Losing yourself in the process might be.

Maybe real balance is not about doing less, but about remembering that our value was never meant to be measured only by what we produce.

And maybe, in the middle of all of this, we need to remember to take care of ourselves a little more gently. To create space for rest, for our loved ones, for the moments that don’t show up on a CV. Because at the end of the day, life is not only about what we build, but also about what we live.

**Do you feel like work has become part of your identity?**
